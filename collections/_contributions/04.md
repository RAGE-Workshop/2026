title: "WebAssembly Beyond the Browser: Cross-Domain Expectations and Integration Challenges at the Edge"
authors:  Dr. Fedor Smirnov
abstract: >-
    "WebAssembly (Wasm) was originally engineered to provide secure, near-native execution within the resource-rich, highly standardized environment of web browsers. However, its core characteristics—hardware independence, lightweight sandboxing, and polyglot compilation—have triggered a growing movement to adopt Wasm across Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) and the constrained edge.
    This talk explores the architectural realities of deploying Wasm entirely outside its original design envelope. Through a cross-domain analysis spanning environments like sensor networks and automotive architectures, we examine the specific use cases driving Wasm adoption, such as safely sandboxing third-party logic or refurbishing legacy C code.
    The requirements for Wasm vary drastically depending on the target environment. This presentation will contrast the unique expectations of different industries, highlighting which Wasm characteristics hold the most promise for the different use cases, while outlining the high-level integration challenges and open research directions that still need to be addressed."
